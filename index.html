Henrique

I study how teachers learn to teach computer science, and what shifts when computing is framed as sociopolitical.

My research sits at the intersection of the learning sciences, computer science education, and teacher education. I focus on critically conscious CS education, studying the ways pre-service teachers navigate critical perspectives of computing inside a CS teacher preparation program. My dissertation is chaired by Prof. Michelle Wilkerson at the Berkeley School of Education.

Research Projects

The throughline of my research is the intersection of the learning sciences and computer science education. My dissertation examines how pre-service teachers come to see computing critically. Through research apprenticeships, I have also studied undergraduate introductory data science and computational modeling in science classrooms.

Dissertation

Ideologies, Motivations, and Identities in Critical Computer Science Teacher Education

Committee: Michelle Wilkerson (Chair), Lisa Yan, Thomas Philip · Advanced to candidacy May 2026 · Expected completion May 2027

Project 01 [2022–Present]

Critically Conscious CS Teacher Education

Dissertation Research
Chair: Michelle Wilkerson

I designed, developed, taught, and now research a CS teacher preparation course built around critical perspectives of computing. I trace teachers' sensemaking from pre-service coursework into their own classrooms, with follow-up interviews across their first three years of teaching.

Project 02 [2025–2026]

Social Science Informed Data Science Education

Graduate Student Researcher
P.I. Lisa Yan
(NSF IUSE CUE-T)

This project examines the design and implementation of a social science informed introductory data science course, and the role such courses can play in the undergraduate curriculum. I led a curriculum crosswalk across institutions, conducted classroom observations, observed cross-institutional instructor meetings, and supervised two undergraduate researchers.

Project 03 [2023–2025]

Scientific and Computational Modeling with Data (MoDa)

Graduate Student Researcher
P.I.s Michelle Wilkerson, Tamar Fuhrmann,
Aditi Wagh & Paulo Blikstein
(NSF From Access to Sustainability)

MoDa is a browser-based, block-based platform where students code computational models of scientific phenomena and compare those models against data. I led the design and classroom data collection for the photosynthesis unit ↗.
